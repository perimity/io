What cyber insurance applications actually ask about MFA.

Published November 8, 2026 · Perimity · 6 public applications

Multi-factor authentication shows up on almost every cyber insurance application. But which MFA questions? We read the MFA and funds-transfer questions on six public application forms and counted.

6 of 6ask about MFA for remote access
5 of 6ask about MFA for email, and for admin accounts
0 of 6ask about phishing-resistant MFA, legacy authentication, or MFA for people who approve payments

AXIS asks about MFA for all Microsoft 365 users (counted as email) and whether access to a privileged access management tool requires MFA (counted as admin accounts). Admiral asks about MFA for critical information, not email by name.

What stands out

The core questions are consistent

Remote access, email and admin accounts are asked about on nearly every form. These are the questions an applicant answers “Yes” to, and the answers are self-attested.

Three important controls aren't asked about at all

None of the six forms asks about:

  • Legacy authentication. Older sign-in protocols can bypass MFA entirely. An applicant can truthfully say “MFA is required for all users” while legacy sign-ins remain open.
  • Phishing-resistant MFA. Two forms ask which type of MFA is used, but none asks for phishing-resistant methods such as security keys or passkeys, which Microsoft and CISA recommend for administrators.
  • MFA for people who approve payments. Five of six forms ask about callback procedures before a payment, which is a human process. None asks whether the accounts that can approve payments are protected by MFA.

“Yes” covers a lot of ground

A single “Yes” to “Is MFA enforced for email?” can sit alongside excluded accounts, admins with no MFA registered, or password-only sign-ins. The question is asked; the evidence behind the answer usually isn't.

In the Perimity Lab, our test company would answer “Yes” to every MFA question above. The verified picture: 2 of 4 admins excluded from MFA, 4 of 10 accounts signing in with a password alone, and a legacy-authentication block left in report-only mode.

Method and limits

  • Six forms, all publicly posted by the carrier or a distributor. We read the MFA and funds-transfer sections of each on November 7, 2026.
  • Small sample. Six forms don't represent the whole cyber insurance market. Some carriers ask more on supplements, underwriting calls or through their own scanning.
  • Forms change. Several of these editions are from 2022–2025; newer editions may ask more.
  • We count what's asked directly. A control that might be implied by a broader question isn't counted.
  • Perimity makes Pulse, which verifies MFA controls from Microsoft 365. Pulse verifies controls; the answers on the application remain the applicant's.
