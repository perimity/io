This month in the Perimity Lab · November 2026

The policy existed. Nothing was blocked.

Published November 8, 2026 · Perimity

The Perimity Lab is our test Microsoft 365 company, set up with deliberate gaps. Each month we change one thing and record what the evidence shows.

What we changed

The lab had a Conditional Access policy to block legacy authentication, the older sign-in protocols that can bypass MFA. The policy was set to report-only. In that mode, Microsoft logs what the policy would have blocked, but blocks nothing.

We switched the policy to On and ran Pulse again.

Before · report-only

47

Weak · Legacy authentication: Fail

After · On

53

Mixed · Legacy authentication: Pass

Scores are Pulse's MFA score out of 100, from 15 assessable checks. Switching the policy back to report-only returned the score to 47.

Why it matters

Ask “Do you have a policy to block legacy authentication?” and the honest answer was “Yes.” Ask “Is legacy authentication blocked?” and the answer was “No.” On an application, both can come back as the same checkbox.

A policy that exists isn't a policy that's enforced. Report-only mode is useful for testing a policy before switching it on, which also makes it easy to leave on by mistake.

Legacy authentication isn't asked about on any of the six public applications we reviewed (see the tally). It still matters to the question that is asked: legacy sign-ins can get around “MFA required for all users.”

What's still open in the lab

  • 2 of 4 admins excluded from MFA
  • 1 admin with no MFA method registered
  • 4 of 10 accounts signed in with a password alone in the last 30 days
  • SMS and voice still enabled; no phishing-resistant MFA for admins
  • 1 payment approver excluded from MFA
