What it checks

Seven questions behind one “Yes.”

The application asks whether MFA is enforced. MFA Analysis answers the questions an underwriter would ask before binding, and a claims team would ask after an incident.

The questionWhat Pulse verifiesVerified answerInsurance impact
Is everyone enrolled?Registration Which users have MFA set up, and which methods they use, from phishing-resistant keys to text messages. 4 of 25 users have no MFA method registered. Likely subjectivity: enroll the remaining users before binding.
Is it actually required?Enforcement Whether a policy requires MFA for each user, or whether it only exists on paper. MFA policy is in report-only mode, so it is not enforced. A “Yes” on the application would be inaccurate, a misrepresentation risk at claim time.
Who is left out?Named exclusions Every account excluded from MFA, by name, and the policy that excludes it. 3 accounts excluded, including an admin. Likely subjectivity: list each exclusion and explain why it is needed.
Are admins protected?Privileged access How many Global Admins there are, whether each is covered, and with what method. 9 permanent Global Admins, 2 using text-message codes. Admin MFA is a common condition of coverage; text-message codes may be questioned.
Did MFA really happen?Real sign-ins Who signed in with a password alone in the last 30 days, not just what the settings say. 6 users signed in with a password alone last month. Shows MFA happened in practice, the first thing checked after an incident.
Is there a way around it?Legacy sign-in Whether old protocols that skip MFA are blocked, and whether anyone still uses them. Legacy sign-in allowed; 1 mailbox still uses it. A way around MFA that can undermine a “Yes” when a claim is reviewed.
Who outside the company can get in?Third-party access Whether guests and vendor accounts are covered by MFA, whether the tenant trusts other companies' MFA, and which apps hold standing access that never uses MFA. A vendor guest account with no MFA; 2 apps can read every mailbox. Vendor and app access is a common breach path underwriters ask about.
